The selected ETF is the one the model chooses at that bad month's end for the following month. The ETF need not have been held during the loss month. ## Measurement and limits

- ETF following-month returns are adjusted calendar-month total returns, before trading costs. They are returns on the ETF itself, not the 65% sleeve's weighted contribution to the portfolio.
- Full-strategy returns are the production daily backtest aggregated to the following calendar month, net of 10 bps per traded leg. They include both sleeves, actual model execution at the first following trading day's close, weight drift, cash returns, and rebalancing costs. The old holdings remain exposed until that execution close. This is why the full-book return need not equal 65% of the ETF return plus a simple fixed sector return.
- Calendar-month ETF returns differ from the exact first-trading-day-close to next-rebalance-close return. The CSV includes both the exact execution dates and that ETF-only gross execution-window return as a separate diagnostic when the exit close is available.
- All matches are reported, including losses. Adjacent event months can belong to the same market episode and are not independent observations. The counts describe this history, not estimated probabilities for November 2026.
- The cohort with a known next rank outside the top two is identified using information already available at formation. It is a descriptive subgroup, not a newly optimized strategy or an independent validation sample.
- The study is motivated by today's QQQ signal. Existing strategy rules were also developed against historical data. This is exploratory, with cached revised prices/FRED data rather than point-in-time data vintages.
- Verification: the portfolio returns exactly match production; all loss-month returns were independently reconstructed from cached prices; truncating future observations leaves the earlier qualifying cases unchanged.
- No production selector, broker settings, or orders were changed.
